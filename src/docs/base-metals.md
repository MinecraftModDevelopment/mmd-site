of the MMD OreSpawn engine. **The OreSpawn versions are not interchangeable.**

OreSpawn.

## What is Base Metals?

Base Metals adds 22 metals and alloys to Minecraft. The list mixes familiar
materials such as copper, tin, lead, silver, nickel, and platinum with fantasy
metals such as mithril, adamantine, and starsteel.

Some are found as ores. Others are made by mixing metal powders and smelting
the blend. The materials have their own recipes, equipment, and blocks, so
they are useful for much more than filling a storage chest with new ingots.

The mod works well in an ordinary survival pack, but it can also sit alongside
larger technology mods. The modern version uses standard Forge tags so other
mods can recognise its metals without needing special Base Metals code.

## From rock to finished material

Most alloys follow the same simple process:

  <span><b>1</b>Find ore</span>
  <i aria-hidden="true">→</i>
  <span><b>2</b>Crush it</span>
  <i aria-hidden="true">→</i>
  <span><b>3</b>Mix powders</span>
  <i aria-hidden="true">→</i>
  <span><b>4</b>Smelt a blend</span>
  <i aria-hidden="true">→</i>
  <span><b>5</b>Build or equip</span>
</div>

### The Crack Hammer

The Crack Hammer is a sledgehammer used to crush supported blocks and items
into powders. Those powders can be smelted directly or used in alloy recipes.

On 1.18.2, the `basemetals:crushing` recipe decides what can be crushed and
what it produces. A Crack Hammer can:

- break a supported block and produce its configured crushing result;
- crush one dropped item by right-clicking the block beneath it; or
- crush as much of a dropped stack as its remaining durability allows while
  the player is sneaking.

In 1.18.2, the crushing recipe decides the output. Base Metals does not assume
that every ore should produce two powders. A pack can add or change recipes
without changing the mod.

On 1.10.2 and 1.12.2, supported ores produce two piles of powder. Those
powders can be smelted directly or combined into alloy blends.

### Making alloys

Alloys give you another reason to keep and process the metals you find. Bronze
combines copper and tin; brass combines copper and zinc; electrum combines
silver and gold; invar combines iron and nickel; steel adds carbon to iron;
and mithril combines silver, cold iron, and mercury.

Making one is straightforward:

1. acquire the ingredients;
2. turn them into the required powders;
3. craft the alloy blend in the correct proportions; and
4. smelt the blend into usable ingots.

The whole process works with ordinary crafting and smelting. Technology mods
can automate it where they support the same tagged materials and recipes.

### Scythes

On 1.18.2, a scythe harvests a horizontal 3×3 area. Each block still uses
Forge's normal break and protection events, so it should respect land claims
and other server rules.

### Materials with character

Several materials also have special effects in 1.18.2:

- **Adamantine armour** grants resistance as more pieces are worn.
- **Cold Iron armour** grants fire resistance when wearing a full set.
- **Aquarium armour** helps with underwater work when wearing a full set. It
  adds water breathing and resistance, and removes mining fatigue.
- **Mithril armour** removes harmful status effects when wearing a full set.
- **Starsteel armour** improves jumping and movement, while held Starsteel
  equipment repairs one point of durability every 200 server ticks.
- **Lead armour** slows the wearer when two or more pieces are equipped.

Some tools and weapons get material effects too. Adamantine deals extra damage
to high-health targets, Aquarium to aquatic targets, Cold Iron to fire-immune
creatures, and Mithril to the undead.

The equipment effects, Starsteel repair, and villager trades can be switched
off in the modern config if a pack does not want them.

## Modern world generation: OreSpawn 4

On Minecraft 1.18.2,
[MMD OreSpawn 4](https://mc.moddev.zone/docs/mmd-orespawn) places the Base
Metals ores. Base Metals provides the ores and their default rules. OreSpawn
loads those rules and handles world generation.

<div class="bm-engine">
  <article>
    <p class="bm-engine-label">Base Metals provides</p>
    <h3>The ores and their defaults</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>ore blocks, drops, tags, recipes, and textures;</li>
      <li>ten enabled ore rules for a fresh installation;</li>
      <li>dimensions, height ranges, vein sizes, and valid host blocks;</li>
      <li>optional Mineralogy rock-family hosts;</li>
      <li>stable rule IDs used by packs and migration.</li>
    </ul>
  </article>
  <div class="bm-engine-plus" aria-hidden="true">+</div>
  <article>
    <p class="bm-engine-label">OreSpawn 4 provides</p>
    <h3>Where the ores appear</h3>
    <ul>
      <li>provider discovery and validation;</li>
      <li>one set of world generation settings;</li>
      <li>fast ore placement and retrogen;</li>
      <li>world creation screens and configuration help;</li>
      <li>pack overrides without editing the mod jar.</li>
    </ul>
  </article>
</div>

The Base Metals provider only describes ores. It does not replace terrain,
create rock layers, or require Mineralogy. With
[Minecraft Mineralogy 6](https://mc.moddev.zone/docs/minecraft-mineralogy)
installed, the same ores can also appear inside Mineralogy rocks.

Fresh installations use Minecraft's copper ore, so the separate Base Metals
copper rule is disabled by default. Antimony and bismuth are available as
materials but do not have default ore rules.

Each enabled rule has its own height range and vein size. Ores can replace
vanilla stone and deepslate. If Mineralogy is installed, they can also replace
its sedimentary, metamorphic, intrusive, and volcanic rocks.

## Legacy world generation: OreSpawn 3

The current Minecraft 1.10.2 and 1.12.2 releases use OreSpawn 3.2.x and its
older plugin format.

For those versions:

- install the correct Minecraft/Forge build of Base Metals;
- install the matching OS3 build on client and server;
- keep OS3's legacy configuration files with the legacy world;
- do not substitute an OS4 jar or OS4 provider configuration; and
- back up a world before changing ore-generation settings.

OS3 and OS4 use different formats. Use OS4 with Base Metals 3.0.0, and OS3
with the current 1.10.2 and 1.12.2 releases.

## Modpack and interoperability

On 1.18.2, Base Metals registers its materials in standard Forge tags. Other
mods can look up a copper ingot, tin dust, or bronze block without adding
special code for Base Metals.

<div class="bm-pack-grid">
  <article>
    <h3>Tags first</h3>
    <p>
      Ingots, nuggets, ores, storage blocks, dusts, plates, rods, gears, tools,
      and armour use the usual Forge material tags.
    </p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <h3>Conditional integrations</h3>
    <p>
      The modern data pack includes conditional support for Mekanism 10.2,
      Thermal Expansion 9, Tinkers' Construct 3.7, and Ender IO 6 alpha.
    </p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <h3>No required geology mod</h3>
    <p>
      Mineralogy is optional. Without it, Base Metals ores still generate in
      vanilla stone and deepslate.
    </p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <h3>Avoid duplicate ores</h3>
    <p>
      On 1.18.2, change or disable the OS4 rule instead of adding another
      generator for the same ore.
    </p>
  </article>
</div>

Mods do not always need a dedicated Base Metals integration. If they use the
same Forge tags, their recipes and machines can often recognise the materials
already.

## Installing Base Metals

### Modern Minecraft 1.18.2

Install all of the following on both the client and server:

1. Minecraft **1.18.2**;
2. Forge **40.3.0 or newer in the Forge 40 line**;
3. **Base Metals 3.0.0**; and
4. **MMD OreSpawn 4.0.1 or newer, but lower than 5.0.0**.

Do not copy MMDLib, Additional Loot Tables, an OS3 plugin, or a legacy
Base Metals fallback generator into the modern installation. The 1.18.2 port
is native Java 17 and uses OS4 as its only ore-placement engine.

### Legacy Minecraft 1.12.2 or 1.10.2

Install the Base Metals release and **OreSpawn 3.2.x** built for the same
Minecraft version. Use Java 8 and the Forge generation appropriate to that
Minecraft line. The current released Base Metals files are available from the
[CurseForge project](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/base-metals/files).

## Upgrading an old world to 1.18.2

The 1.18.2 port includes migration support for Base Metals 1.10 and 1.12
worlds. It can convert saved Base Metals content and recognised OS3 worldgen
settings.

<div class="bm-migration">
  <p class="bm-migration-label">Before you start</p>
  <h3>Open a copy of the world, not the original.</h3>
  <p>
    Keep the untouched world and its old modpack. Moving from 1.10 or 1.12 to
    1.18.2 changes Minecraft's save format as well as Base Metals and
    OreSpawn.
  </p>
</div>

The migration can preserve recognised Base Metals IDs and carry old OS3
settings, including enabled state, quantities, frequencies, dimensions, and
heights, into OS4. During a direct 1.12 conversion it keeps a backup of the
original region files. If it cannot identify an old numeric block ID, it stops
instead of guessing.

A careful upgrade sequence is:

1. make a full external backup;
2. copy the world into a separate 1.18.2 instance;
3. install only the modern Base Metals and OS4 dependency set;
4. start the copy and read the migration report before playing;
5. verify inventories, important builds, dimensions, and worldgen settings;
6. keep the original legacy instance until the converted world is accepted.

Base Metals can only convert content and settings it knows about. Other old
mods may need their own migration support, so keep the backup until the new
world has been checked properly.

## For pack authors and developers

The modern Base Metals jar contains its default OS4 provider at:

```text
data/basemetals/orespawn/provider.json
```

The file lists each ore's rule ID, host blocks, dimensions, height range, vein
size, and default enabled state. Change these through an OS4 pack override
rather than editing the Base Metals jar.

For add-ons and packs, use:

- Forge item and block tags for material compatibility;
- `basemetals:crushing` recipes for modern Crack Hammer processing;
- OS4 provider overrides for ore placement;
- ordinary data-pack recipes and conditions for optional integrations; and
- Base Metals' common config for the four gameplay toggles.

Prefer these tags, recipes, and OS4 rules over Base Metals' internal Java
classes. They are the supported ways to extend the mod.
